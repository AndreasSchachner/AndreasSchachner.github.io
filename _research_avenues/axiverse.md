<div style="width: 750px;">
   <p align="justify">
    String compactifications generically predict a rich spectrum of
    axion-like particles spanning many orders of magnitude in mass and
    coupling &mdash; the so-called <i>axiverse</i>. This makes axions a
    privileged messenger between the string landscape and observation,
    and one of the cleanest ways to confront explicit compactifications
    with cosmological and laboratory data.
   </p>
</div>
<br>

### Fuzzy axions and the string axiverse

<div style="width: 750px;">
   <p align="justify">
    In
    <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12012" target="_blank">arXiv:2412.12012</a>
    we studied fuzzy axion dark matter arising from string
    compactifications and the associated cosmological relics. A compact
    presentation aimed at a broader audience is
    <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02256" target="_blank">arXiv:2502.02256</a>
    (COSMIC WISPers 2024 proceedings).
   </p>
</div>
<br>

### Confronting string theory with data

<div style="width: 750px;">
   <p align="justify">
    Turning axiverse predictions into falsifiable statements requires both
    large-scale computations of string-theoretic spectra and rigorous
    statistical inference. In
    <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00144" target="_blank">arXiv:2512.00144</a>
    we performed Bayesian inference on Calabi&ndash;Yau moduli spaces,
    constraining axiverse parameters directly from experimental data.
    The wider phenomenological context of weakly-interacting slim
    particles is summarised in the COSMIC WISPers white paper,
    <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03433" target="_blank">arXiv:2603.03433</a>.
   </p>
</div>
<br>

### Gravitational-wave signatures of BSM physics

<div style="width: 750px;">
   <p align="justify">
    Related to the axiverse, and to fundamental cosmology more broadly, is
    the question of what gravitational-wave signals can teach us about
    physics beyond the Standard Model. In
    <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01548" target="_blank">arXiv:2303.01548</a>
    we explored how gravitational waves &mdash; in particular at high
    frequencies &mdash; can be used to test BSM scenarios motivated by
    string compactifications.
   </p>
</div>
<br>
