<div style="width: 750px;">
   <p align="justify">
    Extracting reliable low-energy information from string
    compactifications requires a detailed understanding of the UV
    sensitivity of the resulting effective field theories. Systematically
    deriving corrections to the tree-level string actions &mdash; and
    tracking their impact under dimensional reduction &mdash; therefore
    sits at the heart of string phenomenology.
   </p>
</div>
<br>

### α&prime; corrections in F-theory and Type IIB

<div style="width: 750px;">
   <p align="justify">
    In
    <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04592" target="_blank">arXiv:2106.04592</a>
    we studied the systematics of the &alpha;&prime; expansion in F-theory
    and its consequences for four-dimensional effective potentials, with
    direct implications for moduli stabilisation scenarios of KKLT and LVS
    type.
   </p>
</div>
<br>

### Type IIB at eight derivatives

<div style="width: 750px;">
   <p align="justify">
    A sustained line of work concerns the eight-derivative sector of Type
    IIB superstring theory. In
    <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11530" target="_blank">arXiv:2205.11530</a>
    we combined inputs from superstrings, superfields, and superparticles
    to constrain terms at this order. In
    <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07934" target="_blank">arXiv:2507.07934</a>
    we extended the analysis to five-point axio-dilaton couplings,
    uncovering additional structure in the effective action that is
    consistent with what superspace methods anticipate.
   </p>
</div>
<br>

### Ongoing directions

<div style="width: 750px;">
<ul>
<li>Closing the gap between the low-point couplings accessed via
    amplitudes and the full nonlinear structure at eight derivatives.</li>
<li>Tracking the impact of these corrections on controlled four-dimensional
    vacua, in particular for
    <a href="{{ '/research_avenues/de-sitter-vacua/' | relative_url }}">de Sitter constructions</a>
    and Calabi&ndash;Yau flux compactifications.</li>
<li>Extending the systematics to terms involving the 3-form
    <b>G</b><sub>3</sub> and 1-form <b>P</b><sub>1</sub> of Type IIB, where
    superspace methods suggest a rich but largely unexplored ordering
    principle.</li>
</ul>
</div>
