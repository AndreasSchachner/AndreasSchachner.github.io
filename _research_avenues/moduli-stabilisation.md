<div style="width: 750px;">
   <p align="justify">
    A central theme of my research is the construction and systematic study
    of four-dimensional effective field theories obtained from string
    compactifications, with a particular focus on Type&nbsp;IIB orientifolds
    and their vacuum structure. The guiding question is whether &mdash; and
    how &mdash; the enormous degeneracies of the string landscape give rise
    to controlled, phenomenologically viable four-dimensional theories.
   </p>
</div>
<br>

### Moduli stabilisation with explicit data

<div style="width: 750px;">
   <p align="justify">
    Concrete progress on moduli stabilisation typically requires both new
    analytic ingredients and explicit Calabi&ndash;Yau data. In
    <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14624" target="_blank">arXiv:2109.14624</a>
    we developed the systematics of Type&nbsp;IIB moduli stabilisation in
    the presence of odd 2-form axions, giving an efficient analytic handle
    on models with fully stabilised moduli. To populate such constructions
    with explicit compactification data, we compiled a large database of
    Calabi&ndash;Yau orientifolds in
    <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13115" target="_blank">arXiv:2204.13115</a>,
    including the associated D3-tadpole budgets &mdash; a crucial input for
    any flux vacuum search.
   </p>
</div>
<br>

### Flux vacua at scale

<div style="width: 750px;">
   <p align="justify">
    Numerical flux-vacuum searches expose the structure of the landscape
    far beyond what analytic methods alone can reach. In
    <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15525" target="_blank">arXiv:2308.15525</a>
    we constructed new non-supersymmetric flux vacua in string theory, and
    in <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03984" target="_blank">arXiv:2501.03984</a>
    we carried out deep numerical observations of the Type&nbsp;IIB flux
    landscape, producing large statistical samples of stabilised vacua.
    Most recently,
    <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00615" target="_blank">arXiv:2507.00615</a>
    demonstrated that numerical K&auml;hler moduli stabilisation can give
    rise to coexisting flux string vacua within a single compactification
    &mdash; a feature invisible to analytic approximations.
   </p>
</div>
<br>
